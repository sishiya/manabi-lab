    地球をちぎる（計算のしかた）

    地球を約 1.6 万個の粒（1 個 ≒ 400 km）にして、重力・圧力・粘りを SPH（粒子法の流体の計算）で 1 歩 約 10 秒ずつ進めています。推定

    • 層の境目（内核 1221 km・核とマントルの境目 3480 km）は PREM。密度は、選んだ状態方程式（Murnaghan）で重力と圧力をつり合わせて求めたもので、PREM の密度に近い（マントルの底 約 4.9、外核 9.7〜11.9、中心 13.0 g/cm³）が同じではありません。
    • はじめの温度: 地表 15℃ → 深さ 100 km で 1600 K、その下は断熱（Katsura ほか 2010 にもとづく）、核とマントルの境目のすぐ上 約 3800 K（核の側は 約 4000 K。Lay ほか 2008）、内核 約 5400 K（研究によって幅があります）。圧力が抜けると断熱で冷え、くずれ込んで落ちた分のエネルギーは熱になります。
    • 溶け: マントルの溶けはじめる温度（ソリダス）を Hirschmann 2000（低い圧力）と Fiquet ほか 2010（高い圧力）からつないだ近似と比べ、溶けるのに使う熱も入れて「一部溶けた割合」を出しています。
    • 海と湯気: 海は平均 3.7 km と粒よりずっと薄いので、岩とは別の軽い粒（約 3600 個、1 個 ≒ 37 万 km³）で表し、岩の上に乗せています。地面の上を流れる速さは、深さ 3.7 km の水の波の速さ √(g×深さ) ≒ 秒速 0.19 km まで（崖から落ちるときは上限なし）。表に出た熱い岩に触れた水は沸騰して湯気になり、その量は沸騰でいちばん熱が伝わるときの値（限界熱流束 約 1 MW/m²）で数えた多めの見積もりです。湯気の分子の速さは秒速 1 km ほどで、地球から逃げる速さ（秒速 11 km）に届かないので、空にとどまってやがて雨になります（雨は計算していません）。かけらの上の水は、真空で表面が沸きながら凍ります（凍るまでに約 14% が蒸発する: 温度を下げる熱と凍る熱を、蒸発の熱で割った値）。出た水蒸気は秒速 0.3〜0.7 km で、かけらの重力で落ちてきます。湯気の高さは見やすいように 5 倍に強調して描いています。
    • 簡単にしたこと: 岩の固さ（地球の大きさでは重力の力が岩の強さの 100 倍以上なので流体として扱えるが、小さなかけらは本当は形が残りやすい）、鉱物の相転移、地球の自転、空気、水の重さ（海は地球の重さの 0.02%）。重力は「地球」と「かけら」それぞれの中心からの距離で、球対称として計算しています。「遠くへ運ぶ」は、地球の重力が届かないくらい遠くへ運んだことにして、画面では近くに置いています。表に出た所の黒い皮は、溶岩の表面が冷えて固まることからの推定です。

Inter. 183 ／ Lay, Hernlund & Buffett (2008) Core–mantle boundary heat flow. Nature Geoscience 1 ／ Hirschmann (2000) Mantle solidus: Experimental constraints and the effects of peridotite composition.     God’s-Eye Map

    An app that loads real terrain, aerial photos and maps so you can look over everything from the whole Earth down to a single house. Zoom further out to reach the edge of the universe (about 10^27 m), or further in to elementary particles and the Planck length (about 10^-35 m), all with one wheel.

    Controls

    • Wheel / pinch: zoom in and out
    • Left-drag: move Right-drag, Ctrl + drag, or two fingers: tilt and rotate
    • Click: the address of that spot (worldwide) and elevation (Japan only). For buildings, also the name, use, height and number of floors (in cities with PLATEAU data)
    • Living Earth: shift or fast-forward the time (day and night move), city lights at night, yesterday’s satellite photo (clouds), earthquakes, satellites and the ISS.
    • Into the micro world: zoom right down to the ground, then keep zooming with the wheel. Depending on the ground at that spot, the path splits into “rock and sand” (towns, mountains), “water” (sea, rivers, lakes) or “plants” (forests, grass, parks), then on to atoms → atomic nuclei → protons and quarks → the Planck length. Shapes are display stand-ins; sizes are real
    • Into space: from the farthest zoom, keep zooming out with the wheel to go into space: the Moon, the Solar System (real positions), nearby stars, the Milky Way, the Local Group, the large-scale structure of the universe, up to the edge of the observable universe. Tap the ruler band at the bottom to jump to that size
    • Tear off a piece of Earth: from “✋ Tear off a piece of Earth” when Earth is visible in space. Pinch Earth and pull outward to see, by calculation, what happens to the torn hole (it collapses, melts, heats up). Details in “Tearing off a piece of Earth” below
    • Moving city: Toei trains and buses in Tokyo (current positions; between stations and stops is estimated), cars, people and birds (imaginary movement along roads = display)
    • Underground: the ground becomes see-through, and you can dive below it with the wheel or by dragging. Use the highlight buttons to visit the underground malls at Tokyo Station or the pipes under Nagaoka City

    Notes

    Tearing off a piece of Earth (how it is calculated)

    Earth is made of about 16,000 particles (1 particle ≈ 400 km), and gravity, pressure and stickiness are advanced with SPH (a particle method for fluids) about 10 seconds per step. Estimate

    • Layer boundaries (inner core 1221 km, core–mantle boundary 3480 km) follow PREM. Densities come from balancing gravity and pressure with the chosen equation of state (Murnaghan); they are close to PREM (bottom of the mantle about 4.9, outer core 9.7–11.9, center 13.0 g/cm³) but not the same.
    • Starting temperatures: 15 ℃ at the surface → 1600 K at 100 km depth, adiabatic below that (based on Katsura et al. 2010), about 3800 K just above the core–mantle boundary (about 4000 K on the core side; Lay et al. 2008), and about 5400 K in the inner core (studies vary). When pressure is released, material cools adiabatically, and the energy of material collapsing and falling becomes heat.
    • Melting: the temperature at which the mantle starts to melt (solidus) is approximated by joining Hirschmann 2000 (low pressure) and Fiquet et al. 2010 (high pressure); including the heat used for melting, the app gives the “partly melted fraction”.
    • Ocean and steam: the ocean averages only 3.7 km deep, much thinner than a particle, so it is shown as separate light particles (about 3600, each ≈ 370,000 km³) resting on the rock. Its speed over the ground is limited to the speed of a water wave in water 3.7 km deep, √(g × depth) ≈ 0.19 km per second (no limit when falling off a cliff). Water touching hot exposed rock boils into steam; the amount is a generous estimate using the most efficient boiling heat transfer (critical heat flux about 1 MW/m²). Steam molecules move about 1 km per second, short of Earth’s escape speed (11 km per second), so the steam stays in the sky and eventually becomes rain (rain is not calculated). Water on the torn piece freezes while its surface boils in the vacuum (about 14% evaporates before freezing: the heat to cool and freeze it divided by the heat of evaporation). The vapor leaves at 0.3–0.7 km per second and falls back under the piece’s gravity. The steam height is drawn 5× taller so it is easy to see.
    • Simplified: rock strength (at Earth’s size, gravity is over 100 times stronger than rock, so it can be treated as a fluid, though small pieces would really keep their shape more), mineral phase changes, Earth’s rotation, air, the weight of water (the oceans are 0.02% of Earth’s mass). Gravity is calculated as spherically symmetric, from the distance to the centers of “Earth” and the “piece”. “Carry it far away” assumes the piece was taken beyond the reach of Earth’s gravity, though it is placed nearby on screen. The black skin on exposed areas is an estimate based on the surface of lava cooling and hardening.
    • Sources: Dziewonski & Anderson (1981) Preliminary reference Earth model. Phys. Earth Planet. Inter. 25 / Katsura et al. (2010) Adiabatic temperature profile in the mantle. Phys. Earth Planet. Inter. 183 / Lay, Hernlund & Buffett (2008) Core–mantle boundary heat flow. Nature Geoscience 1 / Hirschmann (2000) Mantle solidus: Experimental constraints and the effects of peridotite composition. Geochem. Geophys. Geosyst. 1 / Fiquet et al. (2010) Melting of peridotite to 140 gigapascals. Science 329 / Monaghan (1992) Smoothed particle hydrodynamics. Annu. Rev. Astron.
